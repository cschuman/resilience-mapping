# Health Resilience Mapping: Executive Summary

**Version 2.0 | December 2025**
**Revised following peer review**

---

## The Challenge

Health outcomes in America vary dramatically by geography. Where you live is strongly associated with your health trajectory. Yet understanding why some disadvantaged communities achieve better-than-expected health outcomes remains an open research question.

## What We Did

We analyzed **63,847 census tracts** (population ≥ 500) representing **219.8 million Americans** using CDC health data, USDA food access data, and Census demographics. We calculated composite burden scores and identified statistical outliers that deviate from predictions.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Regional Patterns Replicate Known Disparities

| Region | Avg Burden | Population | Context |
|--------|------------|------------|---------|
| **West** | -0.37 | 56.7M | Lowest burden |
| **Northeast** | -0.17 | 46.1M | Below average |
| **Midwest** | +0.16 | 54.0M | Above average |
| **South** | +0.30 | 63.1M | Highest burden |

**Important:** These patterns are **not novel discoveries**. They replicate the Stroke Belt (documented since 1965), Diabetes Belt (Barker et al., 2011), and decades of Appalachian health research.

### 2. Geographic Life Expectancy Gaps (From External Literature)

We did not link our data to mortality. Published research documents:

| Finding | Source |
|---------|--------|
| 20-year gap between best and worst counties | Dwyer-Lindgren et al., 2017 |
| 14.6-year gap between richest and poorest 1% | Chetty et al., 2016 |
| Rural-urban gap widened from 0.4 to 3.5 years | Singh & Siahpush |

### 3. Statistical Outliers Identified

We identified tracts with better-than-expected outcomes given structural conditions. These represent **hypotheses for investigation**, not validated "resilient communities."

**What outliers might represent:**
- Genuine protective factors (requires investigation)
- Measurement error in model-based estimates
- Unmeasured confounders
- Statistical noise

---

## What This Analysis IS

- **Descriptive:** Documents geographic variation at tract level
- **Hypothesis-generating:** Identifies outliers for further investigation
- **Replication with granularity:** Confirms known patterns at finer resolution
- **Advocacy tool:** Quantifies geographic inequity

## What This Analysis IS NOT

- **Causal:** Cannot prove geography causes health outcomes
- **Predictive:** Cannot forecast future community trajectories (yet)
- **Prescriptive:** Cannot determine what interventions would work
- **Novel discovery:** Regional patterns were already documented

---

## Methodology Grade: C-

Following peer review, we honestly assess our methodology as having significant limitations:

| Issue | Severity |
|-------|----------|
| Temporal misalignment (2019→2023, spans COVID) | High |
| No age standardization at tract level | High |
| Construct validity concern (r=-0.72) | High |
| No external validation against mortality | High |
| Model-based estimates with unquantified uncertainty | Medium |

## Five Paths to Meaningful Contribution

To move beyond description, we propose:

### 1. PREDICTION
Build trajectory forecasting using historical PLACES data (2020-2024). Identify communities **before** they decline.

### 2. MECHANISM
Link resilience to specific infrastructure (FQHCs, broadband, SNAP). Answer "what makes the difference."

### 3. VALIDATION
Partner with outlier communities for mixed-methods ground-truthing. Ask residents what makes them different.

### 4. EQUITY LENS
Analyze whether protective factors differ by race. Overlay historical redlining.

### 5. TOOL VALIDATION
Formal usability testing with local health departments. Publish adoption evidence.

---

## The Bottom Line

**What we know:** Health burden varies geographically in patterns consistent with 60 years of research.

**What we don't know:** Why some disadvantaged communities achieve better outcomes, and whether those factors are transferable.

**What's needed:** External validation, longitudinal analysis, and community-engaged investigation before these findings can inform policy.
