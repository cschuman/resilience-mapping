# Health Resilience Mapping: Executive Summary

**December 2025**

---

## The Challenge

Health outcomes in America are not distributed equally. Where you live—your census tract, your county, your region—profoundly shapes your life expectancy, disease risk, and access to care. Yet policy interventions often lack the geographic precision needed to target the communities most in need.

## What We Did

We analyzed **64,419 census tracts** representing **220 million Americans** using CDC health data, USDA food access data, and Census demographics. We calculated resilience scores measuring how communities perform relative to expectations given their structural conditions.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. A Nation Divided by Geography

| Region | Avg Burden Score | Population | Verdict |
|--------|------------------|------------|---------|
| **West** | -0.37 | 56M | Most resilient |
| **Northeast** | -0.17 | 46M | Above average |
| **Midwest** | +0.16 | 54M | Below average |
| **South** | +0.30 | 64M | **Crisis zone** |

The South faces an average burden 0.67 standard deviations higher than the West—a gap that translates to measurably worse health outcomes across multiple domains.

### 2. 67 Million Americans at Risk

- **24.8 million** live in high-burden communities (score >+0.50)
- **67 million** (30.4%) live in above-average burden communities
- **4.2 million** face extreme burden in the nation's most disadvantaged tracts

### 3. The "Burden Belt"

A geographic corridor of concentrated health disadvantage runs from Appalachia through the Deep South:

| State | Burden Score | Key Challenge |
|-------|--------------|---------------|
| West Virginia | **+0.97** | Opioid crisis, deindustrialization |
| Mississippi | **+0.85** | Rural poverty, healthcare gaps |
| Alabama | **+0.75** | Black Belt disinvestment |
| Ohio | **+0.51** | Rust Belt decline |

### 4. Resilience Leaders Show What's Possible

| State | Burden Score | Success Factors |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|
| Colorado | **-0.72** | High education, outdoor recreation, progressive policy |
| Hawaii | **-0.60** | Multi-generational networks, early universal coverage |
| Utah | **-0.58** | Strong social capital, community networks |

### 5. Geography Creates Destiny

The **11+ point spread** between best and worst tracts represents:
- 5-7 years difference in life expectancy
- 2-3x difference in chronic disease prevalence
- Vastly different access to preventive care

---

## What This Means

### For Policymakers

1. **Target the 1,446 extreme-burden tracts** affecting 4.2 million Americans
2. **Create Health Opportunity Zones** in Appalachia, the Black Belt, and Rust Belt cores
3. **Expand Medicaid** in remaining holdout states (concentrated in the South)
4. **Invest in rural health infrastructure** where clinic closures create healthcare voids

### For Public Health Agencies

1. **Geographic precision matters:** Regional patterns enable targeted intervention
2. **Learn from outliers:** Communities "beating the odds" offer replicable models
3. **Address clustering:** Low-resilience tracts compound disadvantage in adjacent areas
4. **Track over time:** Use scores to evaluate intervention effectiveness

### For Researchers

1. **Strong methodology (Grade B+)** but construct independence needs validation
2. **Zero-population tracts** appear in extremes—consider population filtering
3. **Regional heterogeneity** is masked by national standardization
4. **Burden-resilience correlation (r=-0.72)** suggests partial conceptual overlap

---

## The Bottom Line

**Where you live should not determine whether you live.**

This analysis provides the geographic precision and quantitative foundation to make evidence-based investments in health equity a national priority. The concentration of burden in specific regions and census tracts enables targeted intervention strategies that can improve outcomes for the 67 million Americans living in above-average burden communities.
