## Ohio's Health Divide: Columbus and Cleveland Are 10+ Standard Deviations Apart

*Same state, 140 miles apart—Columbus has 4 of America's top 10 healthiest communities while Cleveland has 3 of the bottom 10*

**[City, Date]** — A comprehensive analysis of community health outcomes across the United States has revealed one of the nation's starkest health divides: Ohio's two largest cities occupy opposite ends of the spectrum, with Columbus neighborhoods ranking among America's healthiest and Cleveland neighborhoods ranking among its least healthy.

### The Findings

| City | National Ranking |
|------|------------------|
| Columbus | **4 of top 10** healthiest tracts nationally |
| Cleveland | **3 of bottom 10** least healthy tracts nationally |
| Distance Apart | **140 miles** |
| Gap | **10+ standard deviations** |

"This is the same state, the same governor, the same state health department," said Corey Schuman, lead researcher at the Community Resilience Mapping Project. "Yet these two cities couldn't be more different in terms of community health resilience. That tells us something important about the structural factors that shape health."

### What This Means

The 10+ standard deviation gap between Columbus and Cleveland represents an extraordinary divergence:

- **Columbus** shows health outcomes far better than its demographics would predict
- **Cleveland** shows health outcomes far worse than its demographics would predict
- The gap cannot be explained by income or education differences alone

### Possible Contributing Factors

Researchers identified several structural differences that may contribute to the divergence:

**Columbus:**
- Major research university (Ohio State) anchors the economy
- More diversified employment base
- Younger population demographic
- Significant recent economic growth and investment

**Cleveland:**
- Historic manufacturing decline and economic disinvestment
- Higher rates of population loss
- Greater concentration of poverty
- Legacy of racial segregation and redlining

### Policy Implications for Ohio

1. **State-level health data obscures critical variation.** Ohio's average health statistics hide the fact that it contains both some of America's healthiest and least healthy communities.

2. **Cleveland needs targeted investment.** The data suggests that Cleveland communities may need focused structural intervention rather than state-wide programs.

3. **Columbus offers lessons.** Understanding what's working in Columbus could inform interventions in Cleveland and other struggling Ohio cities.

4. **Economic policy is health policy.** The divergence between these cities suggests that economic development, job creation, and institutional investment have direct health consequences.

### Beyond Cleveland: Ohio's Struggling Communities

The analysis also identified other Ohio communities with health deficits:

- Several Youngstown-area tracts show resilience scores below -2 SD
- Parts of Dayton show similar patterns to Cleveland
- Rural Appalachian Ohio communities also show significant challenges

### Methodology

Researchers analyzed 53,889 U.S. census tracts using CDC PLACES health data merged with American Community Survey demographics. "Resilience" measures how much better or worse a community's health outcomes are compared to what socioeconomic factors would predict.

### About the Research

The Community Resilience Mapping Project provides open-access data on community health resilience across all U.S. census tracts. Interactive maps and full datasets are available at https://odds.health.
