# Fact Sheet for Journalists

## Community Resilience Mapping Project — Quick Reference

---

## What Is This Research?

We analyzed health outcomes in **53,889 U.S. census tracts** (neighborhoods of ~4,000 people) to identify which communities are healthier than expected given their socioeconomic circumstances — and which are struggling despite advantages.

**Resilience** = How much better or worse a community's health is compared to what poverty, education, and other factors would predict.

- **Positive score:** Healthier than expected (something protective is present)
- **Negative score:** Less healthy than expected (something harmful is present)
- **Scale:** Roughly -5 to +5 standard deviations

---

## Top Headlines

### 1. College vs. Prison: A 4 Standard Deviation Gap

| Community Type | Average Resilience | Interpretation |
|----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| College towns | +2.95 SD | Among the healthiest |
| Prison towns | -0.98 SD | Among the least healthy |
| **Gap** | **~4 SD** | Enormous divide |

**Story angle:** Educational institutions create health spillovers for surrounding communities. Prisons do the opposite. This raises questions about the health costs of mass incarceration.

### 2. The Hispanic Paradox Is an Artifact

The idea that "Hispanic communities are healthier than expected" is misleading. The aggregate statistic (+0.006 correlation) masks huge variation:

| Origin Group | Correlation with Resilience |
|--------------|----------------------------|
| South American | +0.147 (thriving) |
| Central American | +0.060 |
| Cuban | -0.030 |
| Mexican | -0.029 |
| Puerto Rican | -0.017 |

**Story angle:** Policy based on aggregate "Hispanic health" data could miss struggling communities while ignoring thriving ones.

### 3. Texas Border Towns: 2.6 SD Gap Within One State

| Location | Resilience Score |
|----------|-----------------|
| Austin (Travis Co.) | +1.53 SD |
| Border counties | -1.08 SD |
| **Gap** | **2.6 SD** |

Both are Mexican-majority communities in Texas. Same ethnicity, same state — radically different outcomes.

**Story angle:** It's not ethnicity; it's structural factors. The border region needs investment.

### 4. Ohio's Bifurcation: 140 Miles, 10+ SD Difference

| City | Performance |
|------|-------------|
| Columbus | 4 of the top 10 healthiest tracts nationally |
| Cleveland | 3 of the bottom 10 least healthy tracts nationally |

Same state. 140 miles apart.

**Story angle:** State-level health data misses massive within-state variation. Some Ohio communities rival the healthiest in America; others rival the least healthy.

### 5. The 9-Point Intra-County Chasm

Within single counties (same government, same health department), resilience varies by **9+ points**.

**Story angle:** County-level policies may be too blunt. Hyperlocal targeting could be more effective.

### 6. West Virginia Paradox: High Burden, Normal Resilience

West Virginia has America's highest health burden but near-average resilience. Something protective is working.

**Story angle:** What are these communities doing right? Potential lessons for other high-burden areas.

### 7. Health Equity Gap: 0.43 SD Minority Disadvantage

Majority-minority communities average **0.43 SD lower resilience** than majority-white communities. BUT:

- **Washington DC:** Minority communities +1.87 SD advantage
- **California:** Minority communities +0.15 SD advantage
- **Mississippi:** Minority communities -0.89 SD disadvantage

**Story angle:** The gap exists but varies dramatically by state. This proves structural factors — not race — determine outcomes. Equity is achievable.

## Correct Interpretation

**DO say:**
- "Communities with more college-educated residents tend to show higher resilience"
- "The data suggests structural factors are associated with health resilience"
- "This pattern warrants further investigation"

**DON'T say:**
- "College towns cause better health" (correlation ≠ causation)
- "Low-resilience communities should receive less investment" (they need MORE)
- "Race determines health outcomes" (state variation disproves this)
