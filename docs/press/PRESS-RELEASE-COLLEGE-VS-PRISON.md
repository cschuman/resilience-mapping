## College Towns vs Prison Towns: A 4 Standard Deviation Health Divide

*New analysis reveals communities built around education have dramatically better health outcomes than those built around incarceration—even after controlling for income and demographics*

**[City, Date]** — Communities anchored by colleges and universities show health outcomes nearly 4 standard deviations better than communities anchored by prisons and correctional facilities, according to new research from the Community Resilience Mapping Project. This gap—equivalent to the difference between the healthiest and least healthy communities in America—persists even after controlling for income, education levels, and demographics.

### The Numbers

| Community Type | Average Resilience Score | Interpretation |
|----------------|--------------------------|----------------|
| College/University Towns | **+2.95 SD** | Among the healthiest in America |
| Prison/Correctional Towns | **-0.98 SD** | Among the least healthy in America |
| **Gap** | **~4 SD** | Roughly 4 standard deviations |

"This isn't about the students or the inmates themselves," said Corey Schuman, lead researcher. "This is about what institutions do to the communities around them. College towns attract healthcare providers, healthy food options, and economic opportunity. Prison towns often see the opposite."

### What "4 Standard Deviations" Means

In a normal distribution, a 4 standard deviation gap is extraordinary:
- Fewer than 0.01% of random comparisons would show this difference
- It represents the practical maximum range of community health outcomes
- The gap is larger than the difference between the richest and poorest communities

### Key Findings

**College towns show consistent health advantages:**
- Better chronic disease outcomes than predicted by demographics
- Higher rates of preventive care and health screenings
- Positive spillover effects to surrounding communities

**Prison towns show consistent health deficits:**
- Worse outcomes than demographics would predict
- Higher rates of chronic disease and mental health challenges
- Economic depression affects healthcare access and food environment

### Policy Implications

1. **Institutional investment shapes community health.** The type of institution a community is built around has lasting health consequences that extend far beyond the institution's walls.

2. **Mass incarceration has community health costs.** Beyond the direct effects on incarcerated individuals, prison-dependent communities show measurable health deficits.

3. **Educational infrastructure creates health spillovers.** Investment in higher education may produce community health benefits beyond workforce development.

4. **Economic diversification matters.** Communities dependent on a single institution—especially correctional facilities—may benefit from diversification strategies.

### Methodology

Researchers analyzed health outcomes in 53,889 U.S. census tracts using CDC PLACES data merged with American Community Survey demographics. "Resilience" was calculated as the difference between actual and predicted health outcomes, controlling for poverty, education, and other socioeconomic factors. Communities were classified as college or prison towns based on institutional presence and population characteristics.

### About the Research

The Community Resilience Mapping Project analyzes health outcomes across all U.S. census tracts to identify communities that outperform or underperform their socioeconomic circumstances. All data and methodology are freely available at https://odds.health.
