## 🎯 CONTENT MISSION

**We transform data into stories that inspire action while preserving dignity and community ownership of narratives.**

---

## 📐 CONTENT ARCHITECTURE

### **Three-Site Content Strategy**

#### **STORIES SITE**: Narrative & Emotional
- **Tone**: Hopeful, authentic, respectful
- **Format**: Stories, photos, videos, testimonials
- **Length**: 2-5 minutes reading time
- **Voice**: Community members speaking directly

#### **RESEARCH SITE**: Analytical & Academic  
- **Tone**: Scholarly, precise, transparent
- **Format**: Data, methodology, analysis, citations
- **Length**: Comprehensive with executive summaries
- **Voice**: Researchers explaining findings

#### **POLICY SITE**: Actionable & Urgent
- **Tone**: Clear, decisive, evidence-based
- **Format**: Briefs, talking points, action items
- **Length**: Scannable, under 4 pages max
- **Voice**: Experts advising decision-makers

---

## 🎯 CONTENT PRINCIPLES

### **1. Dignity First**
- No deficit language ("food desert" → "limited food access")
- Strength-based framing ("resilient" not "struggling")
- Community members as experts, not subjects
- Beautiful, professional presentation

### **2. Community Ownership**
- Communities control their narratives
- Direct quotes with permission
- Right to edit or remove content
- Attribution and compensation when appropriate

### **3. Accessible Truth**
- 6th-grade reading level for public content
- Multiple formats (text, audio, video, visual)
- Multilingual when community needs exist
- Cultural context always included

### **4. Actionable Insight**
- Every piece of content answers "so what?"
- Clear next steps for each audience
- Contact information for follow-up
- Resources for immediate action

## 📝 VOICE & TONE GUIDELINES

### **Primary Voice Attributes**
- **Respectful**: Never condescending or academic-speak
- **Hopeful**: Focus on solutions and success
- **Honest**: Acknowledge complexity and challenges
- **Inclusive**: Welcome all backgrounds and perspectives
- **Empowering**: Users can take meaningful action

### **Tone Variations by Site**

#### **Stories Site Tone**
```
WARM & INSPIRING
"Maria transformed her neighborhood with one simple idea: 
what if the vacant lot became something beautiful?"

NOT: "Intervention X resulted in 23% improvement in health outcomes 
among low-income Hispanic populations in urban environments."
```

#### **Research Site Tone**
```
CLEAR & AUTHORITATIVE  
"Our analysis of 1,059 census tracts reveals that food access 
alone does not determine health outcomes. Three factors 
consistently predict community resilience..."

NOT: "The data suggests possible correlations between various 
socioeconomic indicators and health metrics in certain populations."
```

#### **Policy Site Tone**
```
DIRECT & URGENT
"Rural communities need mobile markets, not more grocery stores. 
Here's the evidence and implementation guide."

NOT: "Policymakers might consider evaluating the potential benefits 
of alternative food access strategies in rural contexts."
```

## 📋 CONTENT TEMPLATES

### **Community Story Template**
```
HEADLINE: [Action] + [Outcome] + [Location]
"Detroit Gardens Program Cuts Diabetes 23% in Two Years"

HERO IMAGE: Community members in action (with consent)

OPENING HOOK (2 sentences):
"Five years ago, [Community] was known for [Challenge]. 
Today, it's a model for [Success]."

THE CHALLENGE (1 paragraph):
What was the situation? Use specific details and numbers.

THE SOLUTION (2-3 paragraphs):
How did the community respond? Who led? What did they try?

THE RESULTS (1 paragraph):
What changed? Use numbers and personal testimonials.

KEY PEOPLE (sidebar):
Name, role, 1-sentence quote, contact info (if permitted)

RESOURCES (sidebar):
• Implementation guide
• Contact information  
• Funding sources
• Replication toolkit

WHAT'S NEXT (1 paragraph):
How is the community building on this success?

SIMILAR COMMUNITIES (3 links):
Other places doing related work
```

### **Research Brief Template**
```
TITLE: [Finding] in [Context]
"Community Gardens Reduce Diabetes More Than Grocery Access"

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (150 words):
Key finding, methodology, implications

KEY FINDINGS (bullet points):
• Finding 1 with confidence interval
• Finding 2 with effect size  
• Finding 3 with practical significance

METHODOLOGY (1 paragraph):
Data sources, analysis method, limitations

DETAILED RESULTS (2-3 paragraphs):
Statistical findings with context and interpretation

POLICY IMPLICATIONS (bullet points):
What should decision-makers know?

COMMUNITY IMPLICATIONS (bullet points):
What should communities know?

RESEARCH IMPLICATIONS (bullet points):
What should researchers know?

DATA ACCESS:
Links to download data, methodology, and replication code

CITATION:
How to cite this research

CONTACT:
Research team contact information
```

### **Policy Brief Template**
```
ISSUE: [Problem] in [Location Type]
"Rural Health Disparities Persist Despite Food Access Programs"

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (100 words):
Problem, solution, cost, timeline

THE CHALLENGE (200 words):
Scope, current approaches, why they're not working

EVIDENCE-BASED SOLUTIONS (300 words):
What works, where, how much it costs, ROI data

IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP (200 words):
Step-by-step process, timeline, key milestones

RESOURCE REQUIREMENTS (sidebar):
• Funding needed
• Staff required
• Timeline
• Success metrics

SUCCESS STORIES (sidebar):
2-3 examples with contacts

NEXT STEPS (100 words):
Specific actions for policymaker

EXPERT CONTACTS (sidebar):
Who can help with implementation

ADDITIONAL RESOURCES:
Links to detailed research, toolkits, examples
```

## 📊 CONTENT METRICS & TESTING

### **Engagement Metrics by Content Type**
- **Stories**: Time on page, shares, saves, contact requests
- **Research**: Download rates, citation tracking, methodology views
- **Policy**: Brief downloads, contact form submissions, implementation tracking

### **A/B Testing Framework**
- **Headlines**: Solution-focused vs. problem-focused
- **CTAs**: Action-oriented vs. information-oriented  
- **Story length**: 2-minute vs. 5-minute versions
- **Data presentation**: Charts vs. narrative vs. bullets

### **Content Performance Goals**
- **Stories**: 70% complete story reads, 20% save rate
- **Research**: 60% methodology view rate, 40% data download
- **Policy**: 80% brief completion, 30% contact conversion

## 🎯 MULTILINGUAL CONTENT STRATEGY

- **Professional translation** for key content

## 🎯 CONTENT CALENDAR & PRODUCTION

### **Publishing Rhythm**
- **New community stories**: 2 per week
- **Research updates**: Monthly
- **Policy briefs**: Quarterly
- **Community spotlights**: Weekly
- **Data refreshes**: Bi-annually

### **Production Workflow**
1. **Community outreach** and consent (Week 1)
2. **Story collection** and interviews (Week 2)  
3. **Content creation** and fact-checking (Week 3)
4. **Community review** and approval (Week 4)
5. **Publication** and promotion (Week 5)

### **Quality Control Checklist**
- [ ] Community consent obtained
- [ ] Facts verified with multiple sources
- [ ] Reading level appropriate for audience
- [ ] Accessibility standards met
- [ ] Cultural sensitivity reviewed
- [ ] Legal compliance confirmed
- [ ] SEO optimization applied
- [ ] Social media versions created

---

## 🎯 CONTENT GOVERNANCE

### **Editorial Standards**
- **Fact-checking**: Every statistic verified with source
- **Attribution**: Clear citations for all claims
- **Updates**: Content reviewed annually for accuracy
- **Corrections**: Transparent correction policy

### **Community Review Process**
1. **Draft review** by featured community (72 hours)
2. **Revisions** based on community feedback
3. **Final approval** from community representative
4. **Ongoing rights** to request changes or removal

### **Legal Compliance**
- **Copyright**: All images/videos properly licensed
- **Privacy**: No personally identifiable information without consent
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AA standards for all content
- **Accuracy**: Regular fact-checking and updates
